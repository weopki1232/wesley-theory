The unit of the mind · part 1 of 4

First, the soma

The brain's ~86 billion neurons are all built from the same four parts. Watch one assemble itself from the dust, piece by piece — starting with the cell body.

  • SomaDecideThe cell body. It holds the nucleus and sums every incoming signal to decide whether the neuron will fire.

A single neuron · part 2 of 4

Then the dendrites

Branched antennae grow out of the soma. They are the neuron's ears — reaching out to collect signals from thousands of other cells at once.

  • DendritesReceiveThe more they branch, the more connections the neuron can listen to.

A single neuron · part 3 of 4

Next, the axon

A single long fibre shoots out from the soma. This is the output cable that carries the neuron's signal away — in some cells nearly a metre long.

  • AxonTransmitOne per neuron. Soon it will be wrapped in myelin so the signal can travel fast.

A single neuron · part 4 of 4

And the terminals

The axon splits into branched endings — the neuron's mouth, where it hands its signal to the next cell. The unit is complete.

  • TerminalsSendEach ending forms a synapse onto another neuron, a muscle, or a gland.

The support that makes it work

Glia gather round

A neuron never works alone. Scroll, and five kinds of support cell condense out of the dust one at a time — each sends a strand to the neuron and gives it a new ability. There is roughly one glial cell for every neuron.

  • AstrocyteSustainFeeds the neuron, tunes its synapses, builds the blood–brain barrier. Gives it a fed, stable home.
  • MicrogliaDefendThe brain's immune cell: patrols, clears debris, prunes weak synapses. Gives it protection and clean wiring.
  • OligodendrocyteInsulate · CNSWraps axons in the brain and spinal cord with fatty myelin. Gives it fast, node-to-node signals.
  • Schwann cellInsulate · PNSSame myelin job, but out in the body's nerves — one cell per segment. Gives it fast signals in peripheral nerves.
  • EpendymalLineLines the fluid-filled ventricles and circulates cerebrospinal fluid. Gives it a cushioned, bathed environment.

Membrane potential vs. time
Depolarization: voltage-gated Na⁺ channels open, Na⁺ floods in. Repolarization: Na⁺ channels close, K⁺ channels open and K⁺ leaves, restoring rest.

Zoom in · the cell membrane

Inside the membrane

Push all the way in and the axon's dots rearrange into its wall of fat — the phospholipid bilayer: two beaded rows of heads with tails tucked inward, studded with protein channels. Watch the Na⁺ and K⁺ ions stream through those channels, across the membrane.

  • Head rowsFace waterTwo layers of phosphate heads line the outside and inside surfaces.
  • ChannelsCrossHourglass proteins with a central pore let specific ions through.
  • Na⁺/K⁺ pumpCharge itBurns ATP: 3 Na⁺ out for every 2 K⁺ in — holds the inside at −70 mV, loaded to fire.

→ the bilayer holds face-on while the ions flow; scroll on and it reforms back into the whole neuron.

Cell speaks to cell

Across the synapse

The spike reaches the terminal but can't jump the gap. It releases chemical messengers (neurotransmitters) across a cleft about 20 nm wide.

  • VesiclesPackageVoltage triggers fusion; neurotransmitter spills into the cleft.
  • MessengerCrossAcetylcholine, glutamate, GABA, dopamine: each carries a different instruction.
  • ReceptorsReceiveBinding opens channels on the next dendrite, and the decision begins again.

Wire billions of these cells together, each reaching thousands of others, and something appears that no single neuron contains: a mind. Now watch it take shape, one region at a time.

Assembling · part 1 of 4 · hindbrain

First, the life-support

The oldest brain arrives first: the parts that keep you alive without a thought. Watch them light up at the base.

  • CerebellumCoordinateBalance, posture, smooth movement and motor learning (implicit memory).
  • PonsBridgeRelays between cerebrum and cerebellum; helps drive breathing.
  • MedullaSustainControls heartbeat, breathing and reflexes like swallowing, automatically.

Assembling · part 2 of 4 · midbrain

The relay of reflexes

A small hub grows above the brainstem: the midbrain, or optic lobe. It snaps your attention to what you see and hear.

  • Optic lobeReflexSuperior colliculi handle visual reflexes; inferior colliculi handle auditory reflexes.

Assembling · part 3 of 4 · forebrain core

The core lights up

Deep in the centre, the relay and control structures take their place: the parts that route your senses and hold your feelings and memories.

  • ThalamusRouteThe grand relay: nearly all sensory signals (except smell) pass through here.
  • HypothalamusRegulateRuns the body clock, hunger, thirst, temperature; commands the pituitary gland.
  • Limbic systemFeelAmygdala drives emotion and fear; hippocampus turns recent events into memory.

Assembling · part 4 of 4 · cerebrum

And the cortex wraps over

Last and newest, the cerebrum folds over everything: two hemispheres joined by the corpus callosum, split into four lobes, each a different job.

  • FrontalAct & planMotor cortex, decisions, reasoning, personality and speech.
  • ParietalSenseTouch, temperature and where your body is in space.
  • TemporalHearHearing, language and long-term memory.
  • OccipitalSeeThe entire visual world is assembled here.

But a brain alone does nothing

The central nervous system · in the body

Into the head, down the spine

The whole brain shrinks into the skull, and a spinal cord grows down inside the vertebral column. Brain + cord together are the central nervous system (CNS) — shielded in bone.

  • Spinal cordRelay & reflexCarries signals up and down the body — and can fire simple reflexes on its own.
  • VertebraeShieldStacked bones of the spine armour the cord along its whole length.

The peripheral nervous system · from the brain

Twelve cranial pairs

Twelve nerve pairs leave the underside of the brain — one on the left, one on the right — each running to a target in the head. The tenth, the vagus, reaches all the way down to the heart and lungs.

  • Sight & smellI · IIOptic and olfactory nerves wire the eyes and nose straight to the brain.
  • Face & hearingV · VII · VIIITrigeminal, facial and vestibulocochlear serve sensation, expression and the inner ear.
  • VagusXRuns down to the heart, lungs and gut — the body's rest-and-digest line.

The peripheral nervous system · from the cord

Spinal nerves, by region

At every vertebra a pair of spinal nerves branches off, grouped by region of the spine — each a thin two-way cable carrying sensation in and commands out.

  • Cervical · 8 pairsNeck & armsThe upper pairs weave into the brachial plexus that drives the shoulders and hands.
  • Thoracic · 12 pairsChest wallWrap around the ribs, one pair per level, to the muscles and skin of the trunk.
  • Heart & lungsWhat they reachThe organs the vagus and thoracic nerves quietly keep running.
